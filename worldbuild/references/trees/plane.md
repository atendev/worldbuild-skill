# Plane

A whole plane of existence, demiplane, or other cosmological layer — anything with its own physics rather than a place on a map. If the thing being built is a place *within* a plane (a city in the Feywild, a fortress on the Plane of Fire), that's a Location note that names this plane as its parent, not a Plane note.

## Infobox fields

Asked as an early pass, before the narrative branches below. See `_shared.md` → "How Infobox fields work" for general handling, and "Shared infobox fields" for the Name/Status fields every category includes.

Plane-specific, in addition to the shared ones:

- **Type** — natural / crafted / domain / transitive, from the root decision below. Always included once resolved.
- **Cosmological position** — the tier, layer, or grouping this plane belongs to in the setting's cosmology (e.g. "inner planes," "the elemental ring"). Fact-find first — most settings with more than one plane already have some map-of-the-cosmos note; only ask if it's genuinely absent.
- **Borders / connects to** — neighbouring planes it overlaps, touches, or links to directly. Fact-find first.
- **Includes** — named regions, layers, or features inside it, if any exist yet in the notes. Fact-find — don't ask the user to enumerate children that haven't been written; this fills in as they get linked, same as Location's Included locations.
- **Origin / formed** — when it came to be (absolute date, era, "since before the gods") and, for crafted planes, by whom.
- **Sovereign / patron** — a ruling, founding, or bound entity, if any (fact-find against Character notes). Skip for planes no one rules.
- **Essence** — one phrase naming what this plane embodies or is "made of" conceptually (elemental fire, the waiting dead, dreams, the space between). If it takes more than a phrase, it belongs in the body.
- **Time flow** — how time passes here relative to the setting's baseline world. Skip if it's the same — don't render "normal."
- **Native inhabitants** — species or creature types native to the plane; link to existing Species/Creature notes rather than describing them here.
- **Access** — condensed to a phrase ("by death, or the Veil of the Cairn"); the full explanation goes in Entry & Exit.

## Root decision — type gate

What kind of plane is this? The type-specific branches below depend on it.

- **Natural / primordial** — exists as part of reality's basic structure; nobody made it.
- **Crafted / demiplane** — deliberately made by someone (a god, a wizard, a ritual, an accident with a maker).
- **Domain** — shaped by and answering to a single sovereign's will, whether or not they made it.
- **Transitive** — a between-space mainly crossed rather than lived in, connecting other planes.

A plane can plausibly be two of these (a crafted plane that is also its maker's domain). Pick the one that drives its story and borrow the other's questions only if they genuinely add something. If none fits, don't force it — flag to the user and ask what the right type is; it may be worth adding one here.

## Core branch (every type)

Interview questions:

- **Nature & laws** — what works differently here than in the baseline world? Gravity, time, magic, death, memory, identity, the senses — ask about the ones that matter for this plane, not a checklist of all of them.
- **Layout** — one continuous landscape, layered, infinite, fragmentary, or shaped by whoever perceives it?
- **First moments** — what does arriving here feel like for someone who's never been? Not covered by any WA field, but it's the part of a plane a story actually puts on the page.
- **Denizens** — who or what lives here, and does any of it have agency in the story? (Check Connections fragment first.)
- **Entry & exit** — every known way in and out, who controls each, and what each costs. Fact-find first; many settings mention entry methods in passing in artifact, religion, or faction notes.
- **Effects on visitors** — what does staying here do to someone over time? What are the hazards?
- **Relationship to the mortal/baseline world** — how does it bleed into or shape ordinary life (ghosts, dreams, seasons, weather, magic)?
- **Belief vs. truth** — what do people in the world believe this plane is, versus what it actually is? Absent from WA's reference-style fields; worth asking because the gap is usually where the story lives.

## Crafted / demiplane branch

- Why was it made, and was that purpose ever fulfilled?
- What was rushed, compromised, or built in as a flaw?
- What happens to it if its maker dies, leaves, or is cut off from it?
- How has it been altered or repurposed since it was made?

## Domain branch

- How does the sovereign's will show up physically or socially here?
- What happens to those who defy the sovereign inside their domain?
- What would happen to the plane if the sovereign fell?

## Transitive branch

- What does it connect, and are any of those connections now broken or restricted?
- How is it navigated — maps, guides, instinct, luck?
- What gets lost in it, and does anything ever come back?

## Natural / primordial branch

- What does it embody, at its most basic?
- What would weaken, corrupt, or unbalance it, and has that happened?

## Mechanics (conditional — tabletop / game settings only)

Only ask if the setting is a game or TTRPG and the plane will actually be visited in play:

- Environmental rules or conditions that apply while there.
- Rules for travel, getting lost, or time passing.
- Any special boons, banes, or conditions it grants.

## Document sections (prose body)

**Overview**, **Cosmological Position**, **Nature & Laws**, **Geography & Features**, **Sensory & Arrival**, **Denizens**, **Entry & Exit**, **Effects & Hazards**, **Resources & Valuables**, **Relationship to the Mortal World**, **History**, **Secrets** (the belief-vs-truth answer and anything else not publicly known — flagged as such).

Type-conditional: **Purpose & Construction** (Crafted), **Sovereign's Will** (Domain), **Connections & Navigation** (Transitive), **Essence** (Natural). **Mechanics** only for game settings.

Skip any section that genuinely doesn't apply (a transitive void may have no Resources).

Narrative function, connections to existing material, tone consistency — in that order, after the type-specific branch above is resolved.
