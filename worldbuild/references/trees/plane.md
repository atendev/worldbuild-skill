# Plane

A whole plane of existence, demiplane, or other cosmological layer. Anything with its own physics counts, rather than a place on a map. If the thing being built is a place within a plane, such as a city in the Feywild or a fortress on the Plane of Fire, it is a Location note that names this plane as its parent, not a Plane note.

## Infobox fields

Asked as an early pass, before the narrative branches below. See `_shared.md`. Its "How Infobox fields work" section covers general handling. Its "Shared infobox fields" section covers the Name and Status fields that every category includes.

Plane-specific, in addition to the shared ones:

- **Type**: natural, crafted, domain, or transitive, from the root decision below. Always included once resolved.
- **Cosmological position**: the tier, layer, or grouping this plane belongs to in the setting's cosmology, for example "inner planes" or "the elemental ring". Check the notes first. Most settings with more than one plane already have a map of the cosmos. Ask only if none exists.
- **Borders / connects to**: neighbouring planes it overlaps, touches, or links to directly. Check the notes first.
- **Includes**: named regions, layers, or features inside it, if any exist in the notes yet. Check the notes first. Don't ask the user to list children that haven't been written. This fills in as they get linked, the same way Location's Included locations does.
- **Origin / formed**: when it came to be, such as an absolute date, an era, or "since before the gods". For crafted planes, also name who made it.
- **Sovereign / patron**: a ruling, founding, or bound entity, if one exists. Check against Character notes. Skip for planes no one rules.
- **Essence**: one phrase naming what this plane embodies or is made of, such as elemental fire, the waiting dead, dreams, or the space between. If it takes more than a phrase, it belongs in the body.
- **Time flow**: how time passes here compared to the setting's baseline world. Skip it if time passes the same way. Don't render "normal."
- **Native inhabitants**: species or creature types native to the plane. Link to existing Species or Creature notes instead of describing them here.
- **Access**: a short phrase, such as "by death, or the Veil of the Cairn". The full explanation goes in Entry & Exit.

## Root decision: type gate

What kind of plane is this? The type-specific branches below depend on the answer.

- **Natural / primordial**: exists as part of reality's basic structure. Nobody made it.
- **Crafted / demiplane**: deliberately made by someone, such as a god, a wizard, a ritual, or an accident with a maker.
- **Domain**: shaped by and answering to one sovereign's will, whether or not the sovereign made it.
- **Transitive**: a between-space, mainly crossed rather than lived in, that connects other planes.

A plane can plausibly be two of these. A crafted plane can also be its maker's domain. Pick the type that drives the story, and borrow the other type's questions only when they add something. If none fits, don't force one. Flag it to the user and ask what the right type is. It may be worth adding a new type here.

## Core branch for every type

Interview questions:

- **Nature & laws**: what works differently here than in the baseline world? Gravity, time, magic, death, memory, identity, or the senses. Ask only about the ones that matter for this plane, not a checklist of all of them.
- **Layout**: one continuous landscape, layers, an infinite expanse, fragments, or a shape set by whoever perceives it?
- **First moments**: what is it like to arrive here for the first time? No WA field covers this, but it is the part of a plane that a story actually shows on the page.
- **Denizens**: who or what lives here, and does any of it act in the story? Check the Connections fragment first.
- **Entry & exit**: every known way in and out, who controls each, and what each costs. Check the notes first. Many settings mention entry methods in passing in artifact, religion, or faction notes.
- **Effects on visitors**: what does staying here do to someone over time? What are the hazards?
- **Relationship to the mortal or baseline world**: how does the plane leak into or shape ordinary life, through ghosts, dreams, seasons, weather, or magic?
- **Belief vs. truth**: what do people in the world believe this plane is, and what is it actually? WA's reference-style fields don't ask this. It is worth asking because the gap between the two is usually where the story is.

## Crafted / demiplane branch

- Why was it made, and was that purpose ever fulfilled?
- What was rushed, compromised, or built in as a flaw?
- What happens to it if its maker dies, leaves, or is cut off from it?
- How has it been altered or repurposed since it was made?

## Domain branch

- How does the sovereign's will show up physically or socially here?
- What happens to those who defy the sovereign inside their domain?
- What would happen to the plane if the sovereign fell?

## Transitive branch

- What does it connect, and are any of those connections now broken or restricted?
- How is it navigated? Maps, guides, instinct, luck?
- What gets lost in it, and does anything ever come back?

## Natural / primordial branch

- What does it embody, at its most basic?
- What would weaken, corrupt, or unbalance it, and has that happened?

## Mechanics, for tabletop and game settings only

Ask only if the setting is a game or TTRPG and the plane will be visited in play:

- Environmental rules or conditions that apply while there.
- Rules for travel, getting lost, or time passing.
- Any special boons, banes, or conditions it grants.

## Document sections for the prose body

**Overview**, **Cosmological Position**, **Nature & Laws**, **Geography & Features**, **Sensory & Arrival**, **Denizens**, **Entry & Exit**, **Effects & Hazards**, **Resources & Valuables**, **Relationship to the Mortal World**, **History**, and **Secrets**. Secrets holds the belief-vs-truth answer and anything else not publicly known, flagged as such.

Type-specific sections: **Purpose & Construction** for Crafted, **Sovereign's Will** for Domain, **Connections & Navigation** for Transitive, and **Essence** for Natural. **Mechanics** appears only in game settings.

Skip any section that does not apply. A transitive void, for example, may have no Resources.

## Then append `_shared.md`

Narrative function, connections to existing material, and tone consistency, in that order. Do this after the type-specific branch above is resolved.
