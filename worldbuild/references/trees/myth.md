# Myth / Legend

WA source: `myth.html.twig`. A myth is an in-world story. It is distinct from Religion, the belief system, and from Culture, the customs, though it connects heavily to both. A myth can exist independent of organized religion, as folklore, an urban legend, or a campfire story, or it can predate the institution that later adopted it.

## Infobox fields

Shared Name/Status plus the following. **Date of setting**, when the events of the myth supposedly occurred. **Date of recording**, when it was first written down, if different. The gap between the two is often narratively interesting on its own. **Spread**, how widely known it is: universal, regional, or one culture's. **Historical basis**: confirmed true, disputed, or purely fictional in-world.

## Document sections (prose body)

**Summary**, the story itself, told plainly. **The Telling**, how it is told or performed, which differs from a flat summary. It might be oral tradition, a specific written text, or a song. **Variations**, the different versions across cultures and regions. Link to the relevant Culture notes rather than listing "Culture X's version says..." without connecting it. **Historical Basis**, what is known or suspected about real events behind it, if any. **Cultural Reception**, how seriously it is taken: literal belief, allegory, or children's story. **Art & Literature**, notable works inspired by or depicting it, if relevant to the project.

## Then append `_shared.md`

Connections matters a great deal here. A myth's point is usually the network of things it touches, such as a species, a location, a historical figure, an artifact, or an organization's founding story. Fact-find broadly rather than narrowly.
