# Myth / Legend

WA source: `myth.html.twig`. An in-world story — distinct from Religion (the belief system) and Culture (the customs) even though it connects heavily to both; a myth can exist independent of organized religion (folklore, urban legend, campfire story) or predate the institution that later adopted it.

## Infobox fields

Shared Name/Status plus: **Date of setting** (when the events of the myth supposedly occurred), **Date of recording** (when it was first written down, if different — the gap between the two is often narratively interesting on its own), **Spread** (how widely known — universal, regional, one culture's), **Historical basis** (confirmed true, disputed, purely fictional in-world).

## Document sections (prose body)

**Summary** (the story itself, told plainly), **The Telling** (how it's actually told/performed differently from a flat summary — oral tradition, a specific written text, a song), **Variations** (different versions across cultures/regions — link to the relevant Culture notes rather than just listing "Culture X's version says..." without connecting it), **Historical Basis** (what's known or suspected about real events behind it, if any), **Cultural Reception** (how seriously it's taken — literal belief, allegory, children's story), **Art & Literature** (notable works inspired by or depicting it, if relevant to the project).

## Then append `_shared.md`

Connections is unusually important here — a myth's entire point is usually the network of things it touches (a species, a location, a historical figure, an artifact, an organization's founding story). Fact-find broadly rather than narrowly.
