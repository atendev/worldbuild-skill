# Subclass (D&D 5e)

WA source: None. Built from the structure of the user's existing homebrew subclasses. D&D 5e only. Use the 2024 rules unless the user says otherwise: every subclass is chosen at 3rd level and gains features at 3rd, 6th, 10th, and 14th level.

## Infobox fields

Shared Name/Status plus: **Class**; **Source of power** (the patron, tradition, plane, or magic system it draws on, a relation to an existing note, fact-find first); **Feature levels** (3/6/10/14 under 2024 rules); **Practitioners** (who teaches or practices it in the world, a relation to a Faction if one exists).

## Type gate: does the class grant subclass spells?

- **Yes** (cleric, druid, paladin, ranger, warlock, and any subclass that adds spells): ask for an expanded or always-prepared spell list, one or two spells per spell level the class can reach. Check the setting's homebrew spells first and propose any that fit.
- **No:** skip the spell list.

## Document sections (prose body)

**Flavor intro** (who these people are in the setting and how they came to the power. If the power source has an entry cost, such as a pact or a sacrifice, say how this subclass pays it).

**In the world** (how common they are, whether they are legal, and how factions and religions in the notes treat them. Fact-find the factions first).

**Features by level.** For each feature: what it does, the action it takes, uses and recharge, and how it scales at higher levels. Ask how the feature plays at the table, not only what it does. Two subclasses on the same power source should still play differently, because their classes do.

**Signature resource** (if any: its name, maximum, how it is gained, spent, and recovered. Ask whether it reuses a resource the setting already defines or adds its own).

**System hooks** (only if the source of power is a Magic system with its own rules, such as an overcharge, a cost, or a corruption track. Ask how this subclass interacts with those rules in a way that fits the class's playstyle. Don't apply one shared mechanic to every subclass on the same system).

**Drawbacks** (any cost, risk, or social consequence built into the features).

**Sensory signature** (what the power looks, sounds, and feels like when used. Check the parent Magic system's Sensory Manifestation section first).

**Balance check.** Not a section of the note. Before the confirmation gate, compare each tier with an official subclass of the same class and flag anything that spikes or lags.
